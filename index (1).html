Quick goals: the course in plain words

In one line: choose the right pipe, valves and material for a fluid, draw it so others can build it, hold it up properly, and make sure it does not break when it gets hot.

Unit 1: Pipe basics

Idea: a pipe is named by its size (NPS) and wall thickness (schedule), and everything is built to codes.

  • Tell a code (rules for a whole system) from a standard (one component), and match each ASME B31 section to its use.
  • Explain NPS, NB, DN and schedule. Remember: outside diameter is fixed, a thicker wall shrinks the inside.
  • Calculate wall thickness by the B31.3 formula and pick a schedule.
  • Calculate pipe weight per metre, empty and water-filled.
  • Compare how pipe is made (seamless, ERW, SAW), pipe ends, joints and flange types.
  • Pick a flange class from the pressure–temperature table.

Unit 2: Valves, relief and steam

Idea: valves control the flow, relief devices protect against overpressure, and steam lines need draining.

  • Name the parts of a valve and say which valve suits which job (gate isolates, globe throttles, check stops backflow).
  • Explain a control valve, its fail-safe action, and cavitation versus flashing.
  • Define the relief terms (set pressure, overpressure, accumulation, blowdown) and do the sums.
  • Size a line from flow and velocity, then check Reynolds number and pressure drop.
  • Explain drip legs, steam traps, a PRV station and condensate recovery. Calculate flash steam.
  • Calculate heat loss from bare and insulated pipe.

Unit 3: Drawings

Idea: each drawing adds detail: blocks, then process, then every valve and instrument, then the real routing.

  • Compare BFD, PFD and P&ID, and say what a P&ID shows and does not show.
  • Decode a line number and explain the line list.
  • Read instrument tags (first letter is what is measured, the rest is what it does) and bubble symbols.
  • Trace a control loop: sensor, controller, valve.
  • Calculate inch-dia and inch-metre from an isometric.
  • List the factors for a plot plan and where pumps go.

Unit 4: Material spec, supports, racks

Idea: one short class code fixes every component in a line; supports carry the weight and steer the expansion.

  • Explain what a PMS is, who prepares it, its three parts and the ten essential items.
  • Calculate corrosion allowance and select a schedule.
  • Pick a material for a given temperature or fluid.
  • Say what each support restrains: anchor, guide, limit stop, shoe, hanger, spring.
  • Calculate support span and pipe rack width.
  • Sketch a pipe rack cross-section, a control valve station and a utility station.

Unit 5: Stress and flexibility

Idea: hot pipe grows; if it cannot bend somewhere, it breaks itself or the equipment.

  • Define design pressure and temperature, and MAOP for pipelines. Calculate hydrotest pressure.
  • List the B31.3 fluid service categories with an example each.
  • Explain sustained, occasional and expansion loads and the limit for each.
  • Calculate thermal expansion, and the stress and force if it is fully restrained.
  • Find the minimum leg or loop height by the guided cantilever method.
  • Do the short sums: flexibility test, bellows thrust, water hammer, NPSH, turbine nozzle check.

If you only have time for three things

  1. Do the numericals. The question bank uses new numbers, and every one is solved below.
  2. Practise the sketches: valves, supports, PRV station, rack, loops, P&ID symbols.
  3. Learn the comparison tables: code vs standard, pipe vs tube, gate vs globe, safety vs relief valve, BFD vs PFD vs P&ID, design pressure vs MAOP.

Question bank numericals, solved

Every [N] question in the notes, with working. Try each first, then open the solution. Pipe dimensions come from Table 1.2 of the notes.

Unit 1

1.12ID and flow area of NPS 3 in Sch 40 and Sch 80; percentage reduction.

Solution

OD 88.9 mm. Sch 40: ID = 88.9 − 2(5.49) = 77.9 mm, area 4766 mm². Sch 80: ID = 88.9 − 2(7.62) = 73.7 mm, area 4266 mm². Reduction = 1 − (73.7/77.9)² = 10.5%.

1.13Schedule for 900 psig with S = 15 000 psi.

Solution

Sch ≈ 1000 × 900/15 000 = 60. Use Sch 60, or Sch 80 where 60 is not stocked. Final thickness still comes from the code equation.

1.14 / 1.15NPS 8, 3.5 MPa, S = 130 MPa, Y = 0.4, CA 2 mm, mill tolerance 12.5%. Seamless (E = 1), then ERW (E = 0.85).

Solution

Seamless: t = 3.5 × 219.1 / [2(130 + 1.4)] = 766.85/262.8 = 2.92 mm. tm = 4.92 mm. tnom ≥ 4.92/0.875 = 5.62 mm. Sch 40 (8.18 mm) is adequate.

ERW: t = 766.85 / [2(110.5 + 1.4)] = 3.43 mm. tm = 5.43, tnom ≥ 6.20 mm. Still Sch 40.

Comment: the lower joint factor raises the pressure thickness by about 17%, but the same schedule covers it here.

1.16Weight per metre of NPS 2 Sch 80 and NPS 10 Sch 40, empty and water-filled.

Solution

NPS 2 Sch 80: w = 0.02466(60.3 − 5.54)(5.54) = 7.48 kg/m. Water = 1000 × π/4 × 0.0493² = 1.91 kg/m. Filled 9.39 kg/m.

NPS 10 Sch 40: w = 0.02466(273.1 − 9.27)(9.27) = 60.3 kg/m. Water = 1000 × π/4 × 0.2545² = 50.9 kg/m. Filled 111.2 kg/m.

1.26Flange class for (a) 12 barg at 150 °C, (b) 18 barg at 250 °C, (c) 45 barg at 100 °C.

Solution

(a) Class 150 allows 15.8 barg at 150 °C: Class 150. (b) Class 150 allows only 12.1 at 250 °C; Class 300 allows 41.9: Class 300. (c) Class 300 allows 46.6 at 100 °C: Class 300.

Solution

2.2660 m³/h hydrocarbon (ρ 780, μ 0.6 mPa·s) at 1.8 m/s; ΔP for 250 m.

Solution

Q = 0.01667 m³/s. D = √(4Q/πv) = 108.6 mm. Next larger size in Table 1.2 is NPS 6 Sch 40 (ID 154.1 mm), following the notes' rule.

NPS 6: v = 0.89 m/s, Re = 780 × 0.894 × 0.1541/0.0006 = 1.79 × 10⁵, fD = 0.0180, ΔP = 0.0180 × (250/0.1541) × 780 × 0.894²/2 = 0.09 bar.

If you argue for NPS 4 (ID 102.3 mm, only 6% under): v = 2.03 m/s, Re = 2.70 × 10⁵, fD = 0.0182, ΔP = 0.71 bar over 250 m (0.28 bar/100 m). That is still inside the usual limits, so state your choice and reason.

Unit 1 · Piping fundamentals

Basics

Piping system: pipes, fittings, valves and specialty items that carry a fluid between equipment while containing its pressure. Components: pipe, fittings (elbows LR R = 1.5D, SR R = 1.0D; tees; reducers; olets), flanges with gaskets and bolting, valves, specialty items, supports. Piping is 25–40% of installed plant cost.

EPC deliverables: FEED (design basis, plot plan, preliminary PMS) → detailed engineering (layout, GA, 3D model, isometrics, stress analysis, MTO) → procurement (requisitions, bid evaluation) → construction (spools, welding, NDT, hydrotest) → commissioning (flushing, leak tests, as-built).

Codes and standards

Code: rules for a whole system (design to testing), mandatory once adopted, "shall" language. Standard: dimensions and ratings of one component so parts interchange. Codes: B31.3, B31.1, BPVC Sec. VIII, IBR. Standards: B16.5, B36.10M, API 6D, ASTM A106.

B31.1 power, B31.3 process, B31.4 liquid pipelines, B31.5 refrigeration, B31.8 gas transmission, B31.9 building services, B31.12 hydrogen.

B36.10M/19M pipe dimensions. B16.5 flanges NPS ½–24; B16.47 NPS 26–60. B16.9 butt-weld fittings; B16.11 socket-weld and threaded fittings. B16.20/21 gaskets. B16.34 valve ratings. ISA-5.1 instrument symbols.

Design principles

Steps: material selection → line sizing → component selection → layout → stress analysis → supports. Principles: safety first; shortest practical route with flexibility; group lines on racks at consistent elevations; accessibility; high-point vents and low-point drains; no pockets in suction lines; respect nozzle loads; economy; order.

Size and schedule

  • IPS: old system, size about equal to ID. NPS: dimensionless name. NB: British/Indian equivalent. DN: metric, about 25 × NPS.
  • OD is fixed for a given NPS so the same flanges, fittings and supports fit every schedule. ID = OD − 2t.
  • NPS 12 and below: OD is larger than NPS. NPS 14 and above: OD in inches equals NPS.
  • Higher schedule: thicker wall, smaller ID, higher pressure, more weight and cost.
  • STD = Sch 40 up to NPS 10. XS = Sch 80 up to NPS 8. XXS is thicker than Sch 160 up to NPS 6.
  • Mill tolerance −12.5%: actual wall may be 0.875 of nominal.
  • SRL about 6 m, DRL about 12 m. DRL means fewer field welds on racks.

Valves

Valve: a device that starts, stops, regulates or directs flow by opening, closing or partly obstructing a passage. Functions: isolation, throttling, non-return, pressure regulation, relief, diverting, drain/vent, emergency shutdown.

  • Ball: floating (small) or trunnion-mounted (large, high pressure). Fire-safe means a secondary metal seat seals if the soft seat burns (API 607).
  • Butterfly: concentric (rubber-lined, water), double offset, triple offset (metal seat, zero leakage).

  • Check valves: swing (low ΔP, slow, can slam), lift (high ΔP, small bore), dual-plate (fast, non-slam, compact).

Selection criteria: function; fluid; pressure and temperature; pressure drop and flow direction; operating conditions (frequency, access, fire-safe); end connections and size; maintenance and cost.

Control valves

A power-actuated throttling valve that changes flow area on a controller signal: the final control element. Parts: body, trim (plug, seat ring, cage), actuator (spring and diaphragm), positioner (compares signal with stem position and adjusts air until they match), accessories.

Characteristics: quick opening (on–off), linear (level, constant ΔP), equal percentage (most common, varying ΔP). Kv = Q √(SG/ΔP); Cv = 1.156 Kv.

Fail-close (air to open): fuel gas, steam to reboiler, reactor feed. Fail-open (air to close): cooling water, minimum-flow recycle, depressuring to flare. Fail-last where neither is safe.

Flashing: pressure falls below vapour pressure and stays there; smooth polished erosion. Cavitation: pressure dips below vapour pressure then recovers; bubbles collapse; pitting, gravel noise, vibration. Remedies: multi-stage trim, hardened trim, angle valve, staged letdown, place valve at higher pressure.

Control station: block valves both sides (gate, line size), bypass with globe valve, concentric reducers, bleed/drain valves between blocks and control valve.

Eccentric reducer flat side up at pump suction, so no vapour pocket forms.

Pressure relief

Relief events: external fire, blocked outlet, cooling failure, control valve failure, thermal expansion of blocked-in liquid, runaway reaction, tube rupture, power failure, operator error. Lines of defence: inherently safe design → passive → active (alarms, interlocks) → relief.

  • MAWP: maximum allowable working pressure of the vessel.
  • Set pressure: inlet pressure at which the valve starts to open.
  • Overpressure: rise above set pressure to reach full lift, % of set.
  • Accumulation: rise above MAWP during relief. 10% single device, 16% multiple, 21% fire, 3% boilers.
  • Blowdown: set minus reseat pressure, % of set (4–10%).
  • Back pressure: superimposed (before opening) and built-up (from flow).

Conventional valve: affected by back pressure. Balanced bellows: used when back pressure exceeds about 10% of set or varies. Pilot-operated: tight to 95–98% of set, clean service only.

Rupture disc: forward-acting or reverse-buckling. No leakage, very fast, cheap, handles fouling fluids. But non-reclosing and fatigues under cycling. Upstream of a PSV it protects the valve from corrosion and stops leakage; a gauge between them detects pin-holes.

Installation: vertical, close to equipment, at a high point; inlet loss under 3% of set pressure; isolation valves only if full-bore and locked open; discharge to flare header from the top or to atmosphere 3 m above the nearest platform; support for reaction thrust.

Line sizing

D = √(4Q/πv); for steam Q = ṁ vs. Pick the next larger NPS, then recheck v, Re and ΔP. Velocities: water 1–3 m/s, pump suction 0.6–1.5, saturated steam 20–35, superheated 35–60, air 10–20. Darcy: ΔP = fD(L/D)ρv²/2. Fanning: fD = 4fF; fF = 0.04 Re⁻⁰·¹⁶ for steel pipe. K values: gate 0.17, elbow 0.75, globe 6.0, swing check 2.0, entrance 0.5, exit 1.0. Avoid NPS 2½, 3½ and 5.

Steam piping

  • Slope mains 1:100 to 1:250 in the flow direction.
  • Drip legs with traps every 30–50 m, at low points, before risers and valves, at the end. Line size, about 0.7 m deep.
  • Branches off the top. Eccentric reducers flat side down. Air vents at high points.

Water hammer: a condensate slug driven by fast steam hits a fitting. Prevent with drainage, slow warm-up, no sags, eccentric reducers, insulation.

Steam trap: automatic valve that discharges condensate and air but holds steam. Mechanical (density: float and thermostatic, inverted bucket), thermostatic (temperature: balanced pressure, bimetallic), thermodynamic (velocity: disc).

PRV station: separator with trap → isolation valve → strainer → pressure gauge → PRV with sensing line about 10 diameters downstream → larger downstream pipe → safety valve → gauge → isolation, plus a bypass with globe valve.

Condensate recovery: saves fuel, make-up water and chemicals. Parts: traps, return lines, flash vessel, receiver, pumps, deaerator. Flash fraction x = (hf1 − hf2)/hfg2.

Heat loss and insulation

Bare: Q = hπDoL(Ts − T∞). Insulated: Q = ΔT / [ln(r₂/r₁)/(2πkL) + 1/(h 2πr₂L)]. Critical radius rc = k/h; for steam pipes the pipe radius is already larger, so insulation always helps. Insulation reduces loss, keeps steam dry, protects people (surface under 60 °C). Materials: mineral wool, glass wool, calcium silicate, ceramic fibre, PUF for cold. Economic thickness is where the sum of heat-loss cost and insulation cost is lowest.

Dimension centreline to centreline. EL = centreline elevation, BOP = bottom of pipe, TOS = top of steel.

Line numbering and line list

No universal system; each project defines its own. Done by the piping material engineer once P&IDs are well developed. Two methods: by function (origin to terminus) or by size (new number at each size change). A change of line class always forces a new number.

Flow diagrams vs piping drawings

Flow diagrams are schematic, not to scale, undimensioned, made by process. Piping drawings (GA, isometric, spool) are physical, dimensioned, made by piping. GA: scaled plan and sections. Unit 4 · PMS, supports, racks

Piping Material Specification

The project document that defines, for each piping class, the materials, ratings, dimensions and types of every permitted component within stated P–T limits. Prepared by the piping material engineer with the materials and process teams. A piping class is a set of compatible components for a defined service.

Why: standardisation, safety (no mismatches), efficiency, cost; a whole line is specified by one code; basis for MTO and purchase.

Three parts: general requirements and notes; service class index (one line per class); piping material classes (line class sheets).

Class naming (project-specific): A1A = 150#, carbon steel, variant A. Rating letters A 150, B 300, D 600, E 900, F 1500, G 2500. Material 1 CS, 2 LTCS, 3 SS 304L, 4 SS 316L.

Inputs: fluid data; P&IDs; governing code; fluid service categories; design P–T envelope; corrosion data; mechanical requirements (impact test, PWHT, NDT, sour service); component standards; branch rules; valve requirements.

Steps: group services → select material and CA → fix pressure class from the P–T envelope → calculate wall thickness and schedule per size → select fittings, flanges, gaskets, bolting → branch table → valves → notes → review and issue.

Ten essential items: P–T limits; service limitations; corrosion allowance; pipe; fittings; flanges; gaskets; bolting; branch table; valves.

Branch table letters: T tee, E reducing tee, W weldolet, S sockolet, B unreinforced branch weld, P branch with reinforcing pad, N socket or threaded tee, O olet. Olets: weldolet, sockolet, threadolet, elbolet, latrolet, nipolet, sweepolet.

Corrosion allowance = rate × design life × safety factor. Typical: 0 for SS, 1.5 mm CS mild, 3–6 mm CS corrosive. Flange class sets the P–T limits of the whole class; never choose it from pressure alone.

Spec break: change of class along a line, for example at a control valve or PSV outlet. The valve takes the higher class, and the flange bolting to it must also be the higher class. Usually also a line-number change.

Swage nipple for socket-weld or threaded reductions; butt-weld reducer in butt-weld construction. Specials: strainers, steam traps, flame arrestors, sight glasses, hoses, sample coolers.

Pipe racks

The elevated structure carrying process lines, utilities, relief headers and cable trays. Bents at 6 m. Width W = (f × n × s + A) + B with s ≈ 0.3 m, f = 1.5, B 20–25% future. Over about 10 m wide, go two-tier. Tier spacing 1.5–2 m. Area loads 2.0 kN/m² main, 1.5 secondary.

Unit 5 · Design pressure, stress, flexibility

Design conditions

Design pressure: not less than the pressure at the most severe coincident pressure and temperature expected in service. Design temperature: the metal temperature at that condition. Design minimum temperature: lowest expected, sets impact testing. Practice: operating + 10% or + 1–2 bar, at least the relief set pressure; temperature + 15–30 °C.

A system is as strong as its weakest component: lowest of pipe wall limit, flange/valve rating, special items. Short-term variations: 33% over for up to 10 h at a time and 100 h/year, or 20% for 50 h and 500 h/year.

B31.3 scope: materials, design, fabrication, erection, examination, testing of process piping for all fluids. Excludes: 0–105 kPa non-hazardous lines, power boilers and boiler external piping, fired-heater internal tubes, equipment internals.

Stress analysis

Calculation of stresses, forces, moments and displacements under weight, pressure, thermal and occasional loads, to keep the piping, equipment and supports safe and within code.

Objectives: stresses within code limits; nozzle loads within allowables; support loads for design; no flange leakage; limited displacement and sag; no resonance; adequate flexibility.

Hoop PD/2t; longitudinal PD/4t; bending iM/Z. Static loads: weight, thermal, pressure. Dynamic: water hammer, wind, earthquake, PSV thrust. Dead loads: pipe, valves, insulation. Live: contents, snow.

SA = f(1.25Sc + 0.25Sh); f = 1.0 up to 7000 cycles, or f = 6.0N⁻⁰·². Primary stress guards against bursting; secondary against incremental collapse; peak against fatigue. Secondary stress is self-limiting because local yielding relieves it; shakedown means the pipe behaves elastically after the first few cycles.

SE = √((|Sa| + Sb)² + (2St)²), with Sb = √((iiMi)² + (ioMo)²)/Z and St = Mt/2Z.

Critical line list (lines needing formal analysis): connected to rotating equipment; high or very low temperature; large diameter or thin wall; air coolers, reciprocating machines, heaters, tall columns; relief lines; two-phase or slug flow; non-metallic, jacketed, with expansion joints; Category M and High Pressure; subject to settlement.

Information needed: isometrics; line list conditions; PMS data; fluid and insulation densities; equipment nozzle data and allowables; support details; wind, seismic and PSV data.

CAESAR II: 3D beam model. Restraints: ANC anchor, +Y rest, GUI guide, LIM limit stop, YSPR spring, snubbers for dynamic cases. Load cases: OPE (W + P + T), SUS (W + P), EXP (OPE − SUS), OCC, HYD. Output: displacements, restraint loads, element forces, stresses, code compliance report. Workflow: input → load cases → run and review → iterate.

Flexibility

ΔL = αLΔT. CS α ≈ 12 × 10⁻⁶ /°C; SS ≈ 17 × 10⁻⁶. Fully restrained: σ = EαΔT, F = σA, independent of length. Never fully restrain. Elbows ovalise under bending, so they are more flexible (factor k) but have higher local stress (SIF i). h = TR₁/r₂², k = 1.65/h, ii = 0.9/h^⅔, io = 0.75/h^⅔.

Quick test: DY/(L − U)² ≤ 0.03 (in, in, ft), for two anchors and uniform size.

Guided cantilever derivation: Δ = FL³/12EI, M = FL/2 = 6EIΔ/L², σ = M(D/2)/I = 3EDΔ/L². Setting σ = SA gives L = √(3EDΔ/SA). Imperial shortcut L = 6.03√(DΔ). Limits: two terminals, straight legs of one size, legs parallel to axes, elbow flexibility ignored (conservative).

Loops: L-bend (natural change of direction, cheapest), Z-bend (offset), U-loop (four elbows at mid-span between anchors; each leg takes half the expansion). Rack loops are horizontal at the outer edge to avoid pockets and to pass over other lines. Expansion joints (bellows, slip) where loops cannot fit: large-diameter low-pressure lines; an untied bellows needs main anchors for the pressure thrust F = PπDe²/4.

Cold spring: deliberately cutting the line short by a fraction of its expansion. Controls movement and reduces hot reactions. B31.3 gives no credit for stress range and about two-thirds for reactions.

Nozzle checks: NEMA SM-23 turbines 3F + M ≤ 500De; API 610 pumps; WRC 107/297 vessels. Joukowsky ΔP = ρcΔV.

Pump piping

NPSHa = (Ps − Pv)/ρg + H − hf; must exceed NPSHr by about 0.6 m. Cavitation: bubbles form at the impeller eye and collapse; noise, vibration, erosion. Raise NPSHa by raising level or pressure, cooling the liquid, cutting suction losses.

Suction: one or two sizes larger than the nozzle; short; eccentric reducer flat side up; long-radius elbows; 2–3D straight run; strainer; gate valve; no high points. Discharge: pressure gauge → check valve → block valve. Nozzles must not carry the pipe: support beside each nozzle.

Column nozzle growth Δ = αhΔT from the skirt base; support the drop line from column clips.
